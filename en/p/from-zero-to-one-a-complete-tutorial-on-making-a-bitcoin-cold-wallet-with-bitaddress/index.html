Preparation

  • Prepare a computer (preferably a Mac), perform a factory reset, and disconnect it from all networks.
  • Prepare a brand-new USB drive.
  • Paper and pen (ideally, also prepare a metal plate for engraving and securely storing your private key).
  • Another computer with internet access.
  • A VPN.
  • A cryptocurrency exchange account.

Introduction and Usage of BitAddress Bitcoin Wallet

Method 1: Open the website https://www.bitaddress.org/ — it can be accessed without using a VPN.

Method 2: Download the source code from https://github.com/pointbiz/bitaddress.org and run it locally.

Standard Wallet

After opening the page, you won’t be able to click anything at first — don’t panic. As you move your mouse randomly, the system will generate random values, and when the randomness bar reaches 100%, a standard Bitcoin wallet will be created. At the top of the page, you can select your preferred language, and when you click to change the language, the page will not refresh or reload.

SHARE (the QR code on the left can also be scanned for the same result) represents the public key address: 1DsRQ3s7yPfHEGRKyAXBMwyAQduy5yPe6h. SECRET (the QR code on the right can also be scanned for the same result) represents the private key address: L5Eq6n42vVzf7iaAWKaGPRB8NiGiQpHt8qDTkFmGTqowxttBds1U.

The public key is your wallet’s receiving address. When you exchange fiat currency for BTC on an exchange and transfer it to your wallet, this is the address you use to receive Bitcoin.

The private key is the only key that unlocks your wallet. When you need to withdraw funds from your cold wallet to another address, using the private key grants you full control of the wallet, allowing you to send BTC anywhere you wish.

Paper Wallet

A paper wallet is currently one of the most secure and cost-effective options. It uses BIP38 encryption for enhanced security.

The number of addresses you generate depends on your needs. Check the BIP38 encryption option and set a password yourself — you must remember this password. If you forget it, you will not be able to decrypt the cold wallet later even if you still have the key. Then click the print button on the right and save the output as a PDF.

Paper wallet decryption

Here’s an explanation: First, in the wallet details section, enter your own private key and then input the BIP38 passphrase. The key that is parsed will look completely different from the original — don’t panic. The parsed key is the one that can actually be used.

A question may arise here: there are three QR codes and multiple keys, which one is used to unlock the wallet?

Generally, cold wallet addresses for sending cryptocurrency use the compressed format, so we default to using the compressed format. The corresponding key here is the second one:

Key: L2St6aEwi9VkfcrujhctAgoD2vVppfZKFLd5bEvHrLtNKxVToHge

You can use this key to log in directly on a web3 wallet, or scan the QR code in the web3 wallet to log in — both methods work the same.

Cold Wallet Creation

Download the source code from https://github.com/pointbiz/bitaddress.org and copy it onto a USB drive. Then insert the USB into your MacBook (note: the MacBook must be factory-reset and completely offline — disable all network connections, Bluetooth, AirDrop, and any other interfaces that can connect to external devices).

Then open the file bitaddress.org.html shown in the image below using a web browser.

The wallet generation looks exactly the same as above. I recommend using a paper wallet. Write the private key down on paper (or engrave it on the prepared metal plate). The key must be kept completely isolated — do not take photos of it — keep it cut off from the world.

Then log into an exchange, convert your fiat into BTC, and withdraw (the withdrawal address should be the public key address). The process looks like the screenshot above.

On https://www.blockchain.com/ you can monitor your wallet’s activity using your public address.
